The old study was heavy with the scent of leather-bound books and tension. Five figures stood in a loose circle, the silence hanging thick between them.

“Listen,” Eleanor’s voice was sharp, “I didn’t kill Richard. I was in the library when it happened—ask Charles! He can vouch for me.”

Charles, a lanky man with sharp eyes, crossed his arms. “I was not with Eleanor. I was in the kitchen, making sure the wine was chilled—”

“And how convenient that you just happened to be near the back door when Richard’s body was found?” Clara interrupted, her tone dripping with suspicion. She was a tall woman, always quick to find the flaw in everyone else’s alibi.

“You’re one to talk,” Eleanor shot back, “You were the last person to see him alive. You and your boyfriend, Marcus, were arguing in the hallway.”

Marcus, whose expression had been unreadable until now, shifted. “It was a private matter. I’m not about to discuss it here.”

Clara raised an eyebrow. “Does that mean you’re hiding something?”

Eleanor, exasperated, turned to George, the quiet but observant old man sitting by the fireplace. “Well, George? You’re not saying anything. What do you know?”

George smiled faintly. “I’m just listening. But it’s clear to me... one of you is lying.”
